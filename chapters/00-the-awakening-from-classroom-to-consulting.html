Chapter 0 · The Origin

The Awakening:
From Classroom to Career Consulting

Before the framework, before the projects, and before the language of systems, there was a classroom question.

A spark in the classroom

My fascination with Computer Software Engineering began during my student years. In classrooms, I heard teachers describe the possibility of earning from home through computer knowledge—if only a person could master the skills properly.

One question remained with me: if those opportunities were real, should not the people teaching them also have a relationship with the world beyond institutional income? The question was never born from contempt for teachers. My respect for teaching remained intact. It came from a desire for knowledge to be tested in life, not only explained in a room.

What does it mean to teach a capability if the path from knowledge to real application remains invisible?

From teaching to transformation

That question later returned in another form. Teaching Computer Software Engineering as a Junior Instructor at Khanjahan Ali College of Science and Technology in Khulna, I found that the classroom alone was not enough. The real question was how students could apply what they learned in life: in work, in identity, in economic independence, and in the changing technological world around them.

This was the beginning of Rashik — The Awakening: not simply as a class or an institution, but as an attempt to connect practical skills, digital awareness, career direction, and a deeper sense of responsibility.

The question continues

The work that followed changed in scale. It moved through consulting, digital systems, AI, research, public writing, and projects that would have been impossible to imagine from that first classroom seat. Yet the original question has not disappeared. It has only become more urgent in the age of AI.

When tools become more capable, education must become more than the transfer of information. It must help people turn information into judgment, capability, contribution, and a life they can genuinely stand behind.
