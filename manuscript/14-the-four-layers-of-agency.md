# Chapter 14 — The Four Layers of Agency

Language about creation becomes most useful when it makes our own limits clearer.

This chapter develops a philosophical reading inspired by Quranic ontology. It does not claim to settle theology for every reader, and it does not offer a scientific account of metaphysical questions. Its purpose is narrower: to distinguish different forms of agency so that human beings do not confuse making, arranging, reproducing, and originating.

The distinction matters in the age of AI. A person who builds a powerful system may begin to speak as if capability itself makes them the source of all meaning. A framework of agency can interrupt that illusion.

## Layer one: ultimate source

In an Islamic understanding, Allah is not one actor among other actors. The divine is not simply an amplified version of a human maker. This layer points to ultimate origin, meaning, and dependence.

For the purpose of this book, the practical lesson is humility. No human system creates the conditions of existence from which it operates. A builder inherits language, material, bodies, communities, history, and a world already full of relations. This does not make building insignificant. It makes building situated.

## Layer two: origination

Origination names the introduction of something genuinely new into a field of possibility. In ordinary human work, people often experience themselves as originators when they form an idea, create a new pattern, or begin a project.

The framework asks us to be careful. A human idea may be new in arrangement while still depending on prior language, observation, materials, teachers, and conditions. To recognise dependence is not to deny creativity. It is to remove the fantasy that creativity is self-created.

## Layer three: making

Making is the most recognisable form of human agency. It involves shaping available materials into a new arrangement: writing a book, building a tool, designing a service, forming an institution, or teaching a method.

Making deserves dignity because it carries labour, skill, decision, and consequence. It also carries responsibility. A maker cannot hide behind the phrase “the tool did it” when the maker selected the aim, arranged the conditions, and chose whether to release the result.

## Layer four: continuation

Many systems continue through reproduction, inheritance, and interaction. A family continues across generations. A culture continues through practice. A software system continues through maintenance, forks, users, and documentation. No single actor controls the entire process.

This layer reminds us that a builder’s work enters a world of other agents. It will be interpreted, changed, copied, resisted, and sometimes misused. Responsible agency therefore includes stewardship after release.

| Layer of agency | Human temptation | Responsible response |
|---|---|---|
| Ultimate source | To treat personal power as self-made | Remember dependence and limits. |
| Origination | To claim total authorship | Acknowledge prior knowledge and conditions. |
| Making | To separate creation from consequence | Own the design choices and their effects. |
| Continuation | To abandon a system after launch | Maintain, document, and invite accountable correction. |

## The collision of capability and humility

The more capable a person becomes, the easier it is to mistake control for sovereignty. A builder sees that an instruction becomes output, a model becomes application, a project becomes a public page, and a public page changes how people understand them. The chain of cause and effect can feel complete.

It is not complete. Every action remains inside conditions the builder did not create and cannot fully command. A user may interpret a system differently. A consequence may emerge years later. A hidden assumption may fail. Another person may suffer from a choice that looked harmless at the moment of design.

Humility is therefore not passivity. It is the capacity to keep learning after one has gained power.

## Reader test

Choose something you have made. List what you contributed, what you inherited, who was affected, and what responsibility remains after release. The exercise does not reduce your authorship. It makes your authorship more truthful.

## Closing reflection

The four layers of agency do not tell people to stop building. They tell people to build without forgetting where they stand.

When a maker remembers that power is borrowed, responsibility becomes less like a punishment and more like the natural form of gratitude.
