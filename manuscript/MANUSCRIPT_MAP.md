## Editorial principle

This book is not a chronological archive of posts. Existing articles are **source material**, not final chapters. Each chapter must make one disciplined move: introduce a real question, distinguish evidence from interpretation, connect the question to a lived or documented case, and leave the reader with a practical test of responsibility.

The final work will maintain three visibly distinct modes: **documented evidence**, **authorial reflection**, and **conceptual proposal**. Claims about science, law, history, religious text, or external institutions must be cited or explicitly framed as interpretation. No chapter may turn a metaphor into a factual scientific claim.

## Required case-file format

The case-study chapters must use a consistent structure: **context**, **problem**, **constraints**, **decision**, **implementation evidence**, **what changed**, **what remains uncertain**, and **transferable lesson**. Screenshots, diagrams, repository links, release notes, or DOI records must be captioned, dated, and tied to a source location.

## Visual system

Each finished chapter will have one opening image or diagram, one evidence or case-file visual where relevant, and a closing “reader test.” Visuals must be functional: a systems diagram, an annotated workflow, a source timeline, or a comparison table. Decorative images without explanatory value are excluded from the archival PDF edition.

## Evidence and revision rules

The manuscript will use footnotes or endnotes for external factual claims. Personal observations are identified as authorial reflection. Conceptual models are identified as proposals. Every edition receives a version statement, change log, and a source manifest before PDF generation and DOI deposit.
